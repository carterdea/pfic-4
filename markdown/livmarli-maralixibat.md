## Core facts

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Brand name | Livmarli |
| Generic / active ingredient | Maralixibat |
| Salt / active ingredient form | Maralixibat chloride. Label strengths are expressed as maralixibat. |
| Drug class | Ileal bile acid transporter (IBAT) inhibitor |
| U.S. PFIC indication | Treatment of cholestatic pruritus in patients 12 months and older with progressive familial intrahepatic cholestasis (PFIC). |
| PFIC limitation | Not recommended for a PFIC2 subgroup with `ABCB11` variants causing nonfunctional or absent BSEP. This limitation is PFIC2-specific, not PFIC4-specific. |
| PFIC4 evidence | MARCH-PFIC included PFIC4/TJP2 patients in the broader PFIC efficacy population, but the subgroup was small (n=7). |

## Ingredients and dosage forms

| Product | Active ingredient | Strengths | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Oral solution | Maralixibat chloride, strength expressed as maralixibat | 9.5 mg/mL and 19 mg/mL | The 19 mg/mL solution is used for PFIC. The 9.5 mg/mL solution is for Alagille syndrome and should not be substituted for PFIC dosing. |
| Tablets | Maralixibat chloride, strength expressed as maralixibat | 10 mg, 15 mg, 20 mg, 30 mg | May be used for ALGS or PFIC in patients weighing at least 25 kg who can swallow tablets. |

Inactive ingredients listed in the DailyMed patient information for the oral solution include edetate disodium, grape flavor, propylene glycol, purified water, and sucralose. Tablets have separate inactive ingredients in the tablet label; the dosing decision should follow the current package insert.

## How it works

Maralixibat reversibly inhibits the ileal bile acid transporter in the terminal ileum. This decreases intestinal bile acid reabsorption, increases fecal bile acid loss, and can lower serum bile acids. The label notes that the complete mechanism for reducing cholestatic pruritus is not fully understood.

For PFIC4, the rationale is plausible because the primary problem is tight-junction leakage rather than complete absence of bile-acid export into bile. If bile acids still reach the intestine, blocking reabsorption can reduce the recirculating bile-acid pool.

## PFIC dosing from U.S. label

- Use Livmarli 19 mg/mL oral solution or tablets for PFIC.
- Recommended PFIC target dose: 570 mcg/kg twice daily, taken 30 minutes before a meal.
- Titration: start 285 mcg/kg once daily in the morning, then increase to 285 mcg/kg twice daily, 428 mcg/kg twice daily, and then 570 mcg/kg twice daily as tolerated.
- Maximum daily dose: 38 mg/day for oral solution or 40 mg/day for tablets.
- Tablets can be used in patients at least 25 kg who can swallow tablets.

## Major warnings and monitoring

| Issue | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Hepatotoxicity | Liver test worsening and drug-induced liver injury have been reported. Baseline liver tests and frequent monitoring during the first 6-8 months are label recommendations. |
| GI adverse reactions | Diarrhea and abdominal pain are common; persistent symptoms, blood in stool, vomiting, dehydration, or fever may require dose reduction/interruption. |
| Fat-soluble vitamin deficiency | IBAT inhibition can worsen absorption of vitamins A, D, E, and K. Monitor vitamin levels and INR; supplement if deficient. |
| Fracture / bleeding | The label links these concerns to fat-soluble vitamin deficiency, especially vitamin D and K issues. |
| Propylene glycol exposure | The oral solution contains propylene glycol. Children under 5 years are at highest risk for toxicity, so total daily exposure matters. |
| Hepatic decompensation | Contraindicated in patients with prior or active hepatic decompensation events such as variceal hemorrhage, ascites, or hepatic encephalopathy. |

## Common PFIC adverse reactions

In the PFIC trial label summary, common adverse reactions included:

- Diarrhea
- Fat-soluble vitamin deficiency
- Abdominal pain
- Liver test abnormalities
- Hematochezia / rectal hemorrhage
- Bone fractures

Trial 2 rates highlighted in the label/subagent review included diarrhea 57.4% with Livmarli vs 19.6% with placebo, abdominal pain 27.7% vs 15.2%, increased ALT/AST 17% vs 6.5%, hematochezia/rectal hemorrhage 8.5% vs 2.2%, and bone fractures 6.4% vs 0.

## Drug interactions

- Bile acid binding resins such as cholestyramine, colesevelam, or colestipol may bind maralixibat in the gut. Give Livmarli at least 4 hours before or 4 hours after these medicines.
- Maralixibat inhibits OATP2B1 in vitro. The label notes decreased absorption of OATP2B1 substrates, such as some statins, cannot be ruled out.

## Pharmacokinetics / metabolism

- Minimal systemic absorption; pediatric ALGS/PFIC plasma levels were below quantification in most samples.
- Median Tmax after a 30 mg adult solution dose was about 0.75 hours.
- High-fat meal lowers systemic exposure, but the label says this is not clinically significant.
- Plasma protein binding is about 91% in vitro.
- Mean half-life after 30 mg in adults was about 1.6 hours.
- No plasma metabolites detected; minor fecal metabolites were each/total under 3% of radioactivity.
- Elimination is mainly fecal; after radiolabeled 5 mg, 73% was recovered in feces and 0.066% in urine, with 94% of fecal excretion unchanged.

## Trial evidence

This table focuses on PFIC-relevant maralixibat trials. It does not list every maralixibat study in other cholestatic diseases such as Alagille syndrome.

| Trial | Design | Population | PFIC4 relevance | Main evidence signal | Practical read |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MARCH-PFIC / Trial 2 / NCT03905330 | Phase 3, 26-week, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial | 93 randomized pediatric PFIC patients; label efficacy set included 64 patients with biallelic pathogenic variants | Directly included PFIC4/TJP2 patients, but only n=7 | All-PFIC cohort average morning ItchRO(Obs) change from baseline to weeks 15-26 was -1.8 with maralixibat vs -0.6 with placebo; mean difference -1.2, p<0.0001. Serum bile-acid least-squares mean change was about -157 umol/L with maralixibat vs +3 umol/L with placebo; difference about -160 umol/L, p<0.0001. | Best direct randomized evidence for maralixibat in PFIC4, but PFIC4-specific sample size is very small. |
| MARCH-PFIC primary BSEP cohort | Same trial, prespecified primary cohort | Biallelic non-truncated BSEP deficiency cohort | Not PFIC4, but supports drug effect in a severe PFIC group | ItchRO(Obs) change -1.7 with maralixibat vs -0.6 with placebo; difference -1.1, p=0.0063. Serum bile acids -176 umol/L vs +11 umol/L; difference -187 umol/L, p=0.0013. | Shows placebo-controlled effect on itch and serum bile acids, but it is a PFIC2/BSEP cohort. |
| MARCH-ON / NCT04185363 | Phase 3, open-label long-term extension after MARCH-PFIC | 88 patients received maralixibat in MARCH or MARCH-ON in a public conference analysis; included TJP2/PFIC4 patients from the parent program | Includes the same broad PFIC program, but open-label and smaller long-term analysis sets | Public 2-year conference data reported sustained improvements in pruritus, serum bile acids, bilirubin, and growth markers, with no new safety signals. | Useful durability signal, not the same evidentiary weight as a blinded randomized comparison. |

Additional label context: most Trial 2 patients were already on stable ursodeoxycholic acid (89.1%) or rifampicin (51.6%) at baseline. That matters because improvement on maralixibat can happen on top of older supportive therapies.

PFIC4 read: this is supportive because PFIC4 patients were directly included, but the subgroup was very small. It should not be treated as large PFIC4-specific randomized evidence.
