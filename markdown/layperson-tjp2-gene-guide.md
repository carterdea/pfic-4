## Plain-English summary

`TJP2` is the gene that tells the body how to make tight junction protein 2, also called ZO-2. Tight junctions help cells seal to each other. In the liver, those seals help keep bile in the tiny bile channels where it belongs.

PFIC4 happens when disease-causing changes in `TJP2` weaken that seal. Bile acids can leak back toward liver cells instead of staying safely inside the bile pathway. Bile acids are useful for digestion, but they can irritate and injure liver tissue when they build up or leak into the wrong place. That helps explain itching, cholestasis, and possible liver scarring over time.

The gene result names the cause of the disease. It does not automatically predict exactly how severe the disease will be, how fast it will change, or which treatment will work best.

## How inheritance usually works

Most PFIC4 is autosomal recessive. That usually means:

- A person has two copies of `TJP2`, one from each biological parent.
- PFIC4 usually happens when both copies have disease-causing changes.
- A parent with one changed copy is usually called a carrier.
- Carriers usually do not have PFIC4 liver disease.
- When both parents are carriers, each pregnancy has a chance of an affected child, a carrier child, or a child who inherited neither changed copy.

The exact family risk depends on the exact variants and family testing. A genetic counselor can translate the report into inheritance and testing recommendations for relatives.

## What TJP2 does in the liver

Think of liver cells as forming tiny channels that carry bile. Tight junctions are part of the seal around those channels. TJP2 helps organize that seal.

When TJP2 is missing or not working well:

- The seal between liver cells can be weaker.
- Other tight-junction proteins, especially claudin-1, may not sit in the right place.
- Bile acids can leak toward liver tissue.
- The liver can become inflamed or scarred over time.
- Itching can be severe because bile acids and cholestasis affect itch pathways.

This is different from some other PFIC types where the main issue is a bile transporter pump. PFIC4 is more of a barrier problem.

## What this does and does not mean

PFIC4 is not caused by parenting choices, diet, infection, or lifestyle. Food, hydration, illness, sleep, and medications can affect day-to-day symptoms and nutrition, but they are not the root genetic cause.

The same gene name can still cover a wide range of disease. Some `TJP2` changes may stop the protein from being made. Other changes may leave some protein function. Published reports show that PFIC4 can range from recurrent or milder cholestasis to severe childhood liver disease. That is why objective follow-up matters even when symptoms improve.

## Why the gene matters for care

The `TJP2` result can help the care team:

- Confirm the PFIC4 diagnosis.
- Decide whether relatives should be offered carrier testing.
- Interpret whether the variants are likely loss-of-function, missense, splice, deletion, or another type.
- Decide how closely to monitor liver scarring, portal hypertension, and liver cancer risk.
- Explain why treatments reduce bile-acid stress but do not fix the gene itself.

Current PFIC medicines such as maralixibat and odevixibat do not repair `TJP2`. They reduce bile-acid recirculation from the intestine back to the liver. If they help, that is still meaningful because lowering the bile-acid burden can improve itch and lab markers.

For a step-by-step treatment escalation discussion, see [PFIC4 treatment escalation guide for family discussion](layperson-treatment-escalation.md).

## What to ask the care team

- What are the exact `TJP2` variants on the genetic report?
- Are the variants predicted to stop the protein from being made, or might some function remain?
- Were both variants confirmed to be on different copies of the gene?
- Do relatives need carrier testing or genetic counseling?
- What labs and imaging track whether the liver is stable?
- What is the plan for fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E, and K?
- What is the plan for liver cancer screening in PFIC4?
- What findings would make the team discuss biliary diversion or transplant evaluation?
