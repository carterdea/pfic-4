# A practical PFIC4 guide for parents

Research checked: 2026-11-24. Use this to prepare conversations with the treating team. It does not set medication doses or replace each child's care plan.

HTML companion: [pfic4-parent-practical-guide.html](../html/pfic4-parent-practical-guide.html)

Related: [Combined packet](pfic4-research-packet.md), [research and researchers](pfic4-research-updates.md), [related conditions](tjp2-related-conditions.md), [treatment escalation](layperson-treatment-escalation.md), [source index](source-index.md).

## The parent takeaways I would put first

1. Track each child's course separately. A sibling's good response or difficult year is not a forecast for the other child.
2. Ask two questions about treatment: "Is life better?" and "Is the liver stable?" Better sleep and less scratching matter, but they do not answer every progression question.
3. Keep the exact genetic report and any later parental testing or reinterpretation together. A variant class can guide questions without supplying an individual prognosis.
4. Get the surveillance and escalation plan in writing, including who orders it and who reviews the results.
5. Treat nutrition, sleep, school participation, and caregiver exhaustion as care needs. They are part of the disease burden.
6. Follow root-cause research with specific milestones. A patent or conference presentation does not yet provide a treatment a child can receive.

These are priorities drawn from the evidence review and proposed organization for family care, not a validated PFIC4 decision score.

## Understand what the medicines are accomplishing

The tight-junction defect can disturb both the seal around bile channels and the organization of the bile-draining surface of liver cells. Reducing bile-acid recirculation can reduce stress downstream without repairing TJP2. The [research update](pfic4-research-updates.md) explains the human-cell evidence and treatment studies.

Before a medication change, agree on a baseline, goals, a review date, and a plan for side effects. Include the child's own report when age permits. Ask what would count as inadequate response despite better itching, and what would count as meaningful benefit even if a laboratory result remains abnormal.

There is no established PFIC4-specific serum bile-acid number that guarantees protection from transplant or HCC. Ask the team to explain the target it uses and the evidence behind it.

## A separate one-page follow-up sheet for each child

The table is a proposed visit aid. The care team chooses which measures are needed and how often. Keep dates, units, reference ranges, medication exposure, and illness context with every result. Compare trends, not isolated values.

| Domain | What to record or request | What it helps the team assess |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Itch and sleep | Same simple itch scale, nights disrupted, skin injury, daytime fatigue, child's own words. | Whether symptoms and daily function improve. Itch severity is not a substitute for fibrosis assessment. |
| Cholestasis and injury | Serum bile acids, total/direct bilirubin, AST, ALT, GGT. | Bile-acid burden and liver injury. Low GGT does not mean the disease is mild. |
| Liver function | INR/PT, albumin, bleeding symptoms, interpretation after vitamin K when appropriate. | Whether an abnormality reflects vitamin deficiency, impaired liver production, or another cause. |
| Portal hypertension | Platelet trend, spleen size, ultrasound findings, any varices or bleeding history. | Complications that may matter even when jaundice or itch improves. |
| Growth and nutrition | Height and weight trajectories, growth velocity, intake, stool pattern, dietitian assessment. | Adequacy of absorption and nutrition. Weight alone can mislead when fluid is retained. |
| Vitamins and bones | A, D, E status, vitamin K/INR assessment, fractures or bone pain, prescribed formulation and follow-up. | Deficiency and whether replacement is being absorbed. |
| Fibrosis and imaging | Dated ultrasound/elastography reports and the clinician's interpretation. | Change over time; a single stiffness result should not be interpreted without clinical context. |
| Cancer surveillance | Last test, next due date, ultrasound/AFP plan and named reviewer. | That surveillance happens and abnormal findings receive follow-up. AFP needs age and clinical context. |
| Treatment and access | Actual product, concentration, dose, weight at prescription, timing, missed doses, refill barriers, adverse effects. | Whether a change reflects disease, exposure, tolerability, or interrupted supply. |

Background: [GeneReviews](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK584020/) and [PFIC diagnosis/treatment opinion paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10772241/). The checklist is not an instruction to obtain every test at every visit.

## Get a specific surveillance plan

Childhood HCC has been reported with TJP2 deficiency. This warrants a clear plan without assigning a frightening risk percentage that current cohorts cannot support. Ask:

- Does this center start PFIC4 cancer surveillance at diagnosis, or use another threshold?
- What imaging and AFP schedule applies to this child? Is six-month surveillance appropriate, and why?
- Who checks that an imaging report or AFP result has been reviewed?
- What finding would prompt repeat imaging, MRI, tumor-team review, or transplant discussion?
- Does the plan change after a strong medication response?

Do not wait for visible jaundice or a return of itch to ask these questions. See [natural history and HCC evidence](pfic4-natural-history.md).

## Nutrition is more than eating enough

PFIC Network's August 2026 nutrition guidance explains that ordinary multivitamins can be inadequate when fat absorption is impaired and that water-miscible formulations may improve absorption. MCT-enriched nutrition can help some children, but it does not replace every required dietary fat. A pediatric liver dietitian should individualize calories, protein, fat composition, and vitamin replacement. Do not independently remove fat or whole food groups to try to treat the gene defect. [Laura Bull's nutrition guidance](https://www.pfic.org/network_library/nutritional-management-of-pfic/).

Ask whether growth is following the child's own trajectory, how vitamin replacement will be checked, whether feeding has become stressful, and who can help with product coverage. Dietitian, feeding, and psychological support can address different parts of the same difficulty.

## Medicines: make the household instructions unambiguous

Have the pharmacist check the entire schedule, including vitamins and any bile-acid binder. Both U.S. IBAT labels require separation from bile-acid binding resins. Exact concentration, formulation, storage, administration, and missed-dose instructions should come from the child's prescription and current label. Never infer a sibling's dose from the other child's bottle.

The labels require liver-test and vitamin monitoring and address persistent diarrhea, dehydration, bleeding, and liver decompensation. Ask for a written call/hold/restart plan; do not create one from this packet. [Livmarli label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=64000394-1ef6-4e76-8ba8-11f25ba1b167), [Bylvay label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=151f1d3e-2bf4-47ce-b1be-a892de3258fa).

For two affected children, a practical arrangement is a separately labeled medication list, dosing tools, follow-up sheet, and refill record for each. Ask whether appointments and routine labs can be coordinated while preserving separate clinical decisions.

## Sleep, school, and family life belong in the treatment goals

The 2026 PFIC registry baseline study included 161 people across PFIC and BRIC diagnoses. More severe itch was associated with worse sleep and lower family quality of life. Families also reported substantial financial and care-access burdens. This was a voluntary registry association study, not proof that one treatment solves these problems or a PFIC4-only estimate. [Fischer, Lubke and colleagues](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41609050/).

Practical discussion prompts:

- Ask school staff about bathroom access, medication arrangements, flexibility after poor sleep, missed appointments, and a rest plan. Request an individualized school support assessment if needed.
- Record activities the child wants back, such as sleeping through the night, sports, trips, or concentration in class. These make treatment goals concrete.
- Ask the clinic social worker about care coordination, insurance appeals, travel, financial support, and caregiver respite options.
- Make room for different reactions between siblings. One child may want more information or privacy than the other.
- Psychological support can help a child and family manage a demanding illness. It does not imply that cholestatic itch is imagined.

These are proposed accommodations to discuss, not legal eligibility advice or a required school policy.

## Plan for problems before they happen

Seek emergency assessment for vomiting blood, black/tarry stool suggesting bleeding, sudden confusion or marked difficulty waking, or serious breathing difficulty. Heavy bleeding or severe dehydration also warrants urgent assessment. Contact the liver team promptly for new abdominal swelling, substantially worsening jaundice, unusual bruising/bleeding, or persistent vomiting/diarrhea, following their specific instructions. This is not an exhaustive triage list. [Clinical overview](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK584020/).

Ask the team to document the after-hours number, where to go, which records to carry, how to manage illness affecting oral intake, and when medicine side effects require a call. Ask about immunizations and precautions for the child's current stage, especially if transplant evaluation may become relevant.

## What a second opinion should answer

Bring the genetic report, later segregation or reanalysis results, longitudinal labs, imaging, growth charts, medication-response history, and symptom goals. Ask for a written opinion on:

- How secure and complete is the molecular explanation?
- Is current therapy meeting agreed goals for this child?
- What is the fibrosis, portal-hypertension, and HCC surveillance plan?
- What specific change would justify biliary diversion or transplant evaluation?
- Is there a research registry or TJP2-specific study that fits?

A transplant consultation can clarify future options before an emergency; it is not the same as deciding to transplant now. A transplant replaces the liver but also brings surgery, lifelong follow-up, and immunosuppression. Other PFIC subtypes have different extrahepatic and recurrence issues, so ask for TJP2-specific counseling. See [escalation guide](layperson-treatment-escalation.md).

## Unresolved questions to take to the next visit

- Are both children's exact variants, phase, and updated interpretations documented individually?
- What are the next three measurable goals for each child, and when will they be reviewed?
- Who owns the written surveillance, nutrition, and after-hours plans?
- What findings would trigger a change despite good itch relief?
- Which information is still missing to give a useful individualized prognosis?
