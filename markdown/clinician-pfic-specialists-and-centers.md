# PFIC specialists, researchers, and centers: clinician brief

Reviewed: 2026-05-23

HTML companion: [clinician-pfic-specialists-and-centers.html](../html/clinician-pfic-specialists-and-centers.html)

This brief supports clinician-to-clinician discussion and second-opinion planning for PFIC4/TJP2-associated cholestasis. It is not a ranked hospital list. It separates public evidence signals from inference because PFIC4-specific patient volumes are rarely public.

Scope: this packet is primarily about confirmed PFIC4/TJP2. Broader PFIC and genetic-cholestasis center signals are included only when they help identify programs likely to evaluate a PFIC4/TJP2 case.

## Selection framework

Public evidence was weighted in this order:

1. Direct PFIC or genetic cholestasis clinical/research program.
2. PFIC4/TJP2 publication, disease modeling, variant interpretation, or original discovery role.
3. PFIC trial participation or registry/network role: MARCH-PFIC, PEDFIC, LOGIC/ChiLDReN, NAPPED, TreatFIC, PFIC Network advisory/scientific work.
4. Pediatric hepatology, advanced/transplant hepatology, biliary diversion or hepatobiliary surgery, nutrition, genetics, and HCC-surveillance capacity.
5. Accessible second-opinion or consultation path.

## Recent research summary

| Area | Current read |
| --- | --- |
| PFIC4/TJP2 phenotype | Published evidence continues to support marked heterogeneity. The 2025 missense-variant report describes multiple affected children across consanguineous families with low-GGT cholestasis and variable course; it emphasizes WES-based diagnosis and close monitoring. |
| Mechanism | PFIC4 remains framed as tight-junction/barrier dysfunction rather than a primary canalicular pump defect. TJP2 deficiency can disrupt CLDN1 localization/canalicular tight-junction integrity and permit bile-acid reflux/leakage. |
| IBAT inhibitors | MARCH-PFIC provides randomized all-PFIC evidence for maralixibat and included TJP2/PFIC4 in the supplemental/all-PFIC cohort. PFIC4 n is small. Odevixibat has PFIC labeling and strong PFIC1/PFIC2 randomized evidence, with broader-genotype/open-label support. Recent reviews suggest PFIC4 may be biologically well suited to IBAT response when bile acids reach the intestine, but this remains small-number evidence. |
| Natural history/outcomes | NAPPED and TreatFIC remain central for PFIC outcome modeling, especially genotype-severity, serum bile-acid response, native-liver survival, biliary diversion, and transplant questions. Much of the strongest quantified evidence is PFIC1/PFIC2, not PFIC4. |
| Guidelines | EASL 2024 genetic cholestasis guidelines and GeneReviews support genetic testing, specialist referral, nutrition/vitamin care, pruritus management, surveillance, and transplant escalation. PFIC4-specific recommendations remain limited by case counts. |
| HCC surveillance | TJP2/PFIC4 has published HCC reports. GeneReviews flags HCC surveillance for PFIC4; exact interval and whether to screen all PFIC4 patients from infancy vs risk-stratify by fibrosis/cirrhosis should be center-specific. |
| Patient-centered research | PFIC Network's IMPACT/PATH work is moving toward patient-centered comparative effectiveness research, including medications, surgeries, nutrition, mental-health/itch support, financial burden, access delays, sleep, skin, school, caregiver quality of life, and real-world outcomes. |
| Root-cause therapies | Gene editing/gene therapy remains investigational and currently more visible for other PFIC subtypes such as PFIC3. No approved therapy repairs `TJP2`. |

## Suggested second-opinion packet

- Genetic report with transcript, variant nomenclature, zygosity, classification, parental testing, and any reanalysis history.
- Baseline and longitudinal serum bile acids, total/direct bilirubin, AST/ALT, GGT, INR, albumin, platelets, CBC, AFP.
- Growth, nutrition, fat-soluble vitamins A/D/E/K, INR/vitamin K response, bone health if available.
- Medication trials: UDCA, rifampin, cholestyramine/colesevelam/colestipol, naltrexone/sertraline/hydroxyzine/other itch agents, maralixibat/odevixibat dose and response, adverse effects, diarrhea/dehydration.
- Pruritus/sleep metrics: ItchRO or local scale, excoriations, sleep disruption, school/daytime function.
- Imaging: ultrasound, spleen size/portal hypertension markers, elastography/FibroScan, MRI/MRCP if available.
- Pathology and immunostaining if biopsy was done; include outside slide review status.
- Local questions: IBAT strategy, biliary diversion candidacy, HCC surveillance interval, transplant triggers, study/registry options, and transition-to-adult plan if relevant.

## Unresolved questions

- Current center-by-center PFIC4/TJP2 patient volume is not public.
- Current availability of remote second opinions needs direct confirmation.
- The best HCC surveillance interval for a specific PFIC4 patient remains a treating-center decision based on fibrosis/cirrhosis, AFP/imaging history, genotype, and family context.
