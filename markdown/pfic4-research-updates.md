## What changes the conversation

- There is direct, small-number TJP2 evidence for both IBAT inhibitors. The older packet understated the odevixibat evidence by calling it entirely extrapolated. Neither drug has established prevention of liver cancer or transplant specifically in PFIC4.
- Root-cause research now includes a published TJP2 mRNA patent application with cell-culture experiments. This is a concrete research direction, with substantial work remaining before human use.
- A 2026 patient-registry paper measured the burden on sleep, family life, and finances. These deserve a place in treatment decisions alongside blood tests.
- Recent reports reinforce variability and the need to interpret the exact variants. They do not provide a reliable prognosis for an unstudied variant pair.
- Rare eye findings deserve awareness. Their frequency and relationship to particular TJP2 variants remain unknown.

The sections below distinguish peer-reviewed studies, conference material, patent claims, and ongoing projects. A newly accessible manuscript is not necessarily a new study.

## The treatment evidence that matters for PFIC4

### Maralixibat: randomized inclusion, very few TJP2 participants

MARCH-PFIC, published in 2024, randomized 93 children across PFIC types. Its all-PFIC analysis included seven with TJP2 deficiency. The trial supports improvements in itch and serum bile acids across the studied PFIC population; it was not sized to establish a dependable PFIC4-only effect or a variant-specific response. The primary analysis focused on non-truncated BSEP deficiency. The manuscript's availability in PMC in July 2026 does not make it a new 2026 trial. [MARCH-PFIC primary paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38723644/).

Parent takeaway: a child's sustained response is meaningful, but ask separately about symptom relief and evidence of liver stability. Trial participation by PFIC4 patients does not settle the long-term cancer or fibrosis questions.

### Odevixibat: direct TJP2 reports were already available

Di Giorgio and colleagues reported five children with TJP2 deficiency in 2023. All had reported itch improvement; mean serum bile acids fell from 244 to 38 micromol/L. This was an uncontrolled case series. [Primary TJP2 report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37499899/).

A later Italian prospective real-world study included 24 children, five with PFIC4. It reported improvement across the mixed cohort over six months. Because the earlier report and this study arose from the same treatment-access setting and overlapping investigator group, do not count them as ten independent PFIC4 patients without checking individual overlap. [Di Giorgio et al., online December 2024, journal issue April 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11937657/).

Parent takeaway: odevixibat has direct observational PFIC4 support, although its pivotal randomized trial enrolled PFIC1/PFIC2. This evidence does not establish which IBAT inhibitor is better for PFIC4 or justify switching a child who is doing well.

### What longer follow-up can and cannot answer

MARCH-ON and PEDFIC 2 extensions can describe durability and adverse events during continued exposure. Open-label follow-up, treatment discontinuations, and mixed genetic subtypes limit causal conclusions. For new reports comparing treated children with historical controls, ask whether the PFIC4 results are separately reported and whether disease severity and treatment era were comparable. A surgery-free or transplant-free benefit in PFIC2 cannot automatically be assigned to PFIC4.

## Work aimed at the underlying TJP2 defect

### Patient-derived liver cells

Li, Ogawa, Asai, Thompson, Rashid and colleagues established a TJP2-deficient human hepatocyte model in 2022. It showed abnormal cell polarity, distorted bile canaliculi, and impaired directional bile-acid transport. This extends the simple explanation of a leaking seal: TJP2 loss can also disrupt how cells organize the bile-draining membrane. The model supplies a way to test candidate treatments; it is not a treatment itself. [Primary study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35284810/).

### TJP2 messenger RNA replacement

A patent application published October 9, 2025, names Akihiro Asai and Karis Kosar as inventors and Cincinnati Children's as applicant. It describes synthetic TJP2 mRNA, proposed lipid-particle delivery, and increased TJP2 expression and canalicular elongation in deficient cultured hepatocytes. These are the applicants' preclinical findings. A patent is neither peer-reviewed clinical validation nor regulatory approval. [WO2025212858A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2025212858A1/en).

The November 24, 2026 ClinicalTrials.gov searches described in the source index did not identify a human TJP2 replacement trial. That is a search result, not proof that no private development program exists.

Useful questions for the group are whether there are published animal rescue results, whether the delivery reaches the required liver cells, how long correction lasts, whether repeated dosing is safe, and whether established fibrosis limits benefit. There is no supported timetable for clinical availability.

### A new mouse-model presentation

The November 7, 2026 AASLD program lists Thi Hao Vu presenting liver-specific Tjp2 deficiency in a mouse model with a humanized bile-acid profile, with immune activation and metabolic suppression named in the title. The accessible source verifies the presentation, not the complete experiments, effect sizes, or a successful therapy. [AASLD session program](https://www.aasld.org/tlm-26/mechanisms-cholestatic-injury-and-emerging-therapeutic-strategies).

Parent takeaway: this is a lead to follow for better disease models. It is not evidence that an anti-inflammatory medicine or a metabolic supplement treats PFIC4.

## Newer genetics and complication findings

| Publication | What was actually studied | Practical limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Lal and Indian PFIC Registry, 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38447037/) | 44 children with TJP2 deficiency, divided by missense and predicted truncating variant classes. The two-truncating group had worse outcomes. | Retrospective, small groups. Do not apply that group's survival estimates to a child with a different molecular pattern or treatment history. |
| [Khabou and colleagues, April 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s10038-025-01338-w) | Nine affected children across three families; three probands underwent genetic testing identifying homozygous p.Gly532Arg. Marked clinical variability was reported. | Structural modeling supports a hypothesis about that variant. It cannot establish the effect of every missense variant. |
| [Yu and colleagues, May 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42082186/) | One child with cirrhosis and HCC, TFAM and TJP2 variants, all classified as uncertain, plus tissue findings supporting the authors' proposed combined disorder. | A hypothesis-generating case, not proof that PFIC4 routinely includes mitochondrial disease or that two genes necessarily act together. |
| [Monoallelic PFIC-variant cohort, 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42477309/) | 26 children with single-copy uncertain variants across several PFIC genes. No child met monogenic PFIC criteria on follow-up. | A different population from confirmed biallelic PFIC4. It cautions against diagnosing from an isolated VUS; it does not overturn an established clinical diagnosis. |
| [Latypov, Debourdeau and Meunier, February 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41724238/) | A case report concerning a macular eye phenotype in TJP2-associated disease. | Bibliographic details were verified; the full case was not available for assessment. A separate duplicate publication was withdrawn as an accidental duplicate, not as a retraction of the underlying original case. |

## What counts as a meaningful next finding

For each new paper, ask whether it studied TJP2 patients or another PFIC type; how many independent patients were included; whether it was randomized; and how long follow-up lasted. Then ask whether it measured itch alone, or also liver function, growth, fibrosis, portal hypertension, HCC, and survival with the native liver.

For root-cause work, the next useful milestones are reproducible functional rescue, animal efficacy and safety, a registered human protocol, and human outcomes. None can be skipped by a compelling cell image or patent claim.

## Unresolved questions

- Which exact TJP2 variant pairs predict response or progression? Current evidence cannot answer this reliably for most families.
- Does early sustained IBAT response reduce PFIC4 fibrosis, HCC, or transplant risk over many years?
- Can TJP2 replacement be delivered safely and durably, including in an already scarred liver?
- How common are eye, hearing, respiratory, or neurologic findings in well-characterized PFIC4 cohorts?
- What surveillance schedule best balances early detection and burden for each PFIC4 child?
- Will the groups listed provide remote opinions or accept new research participants? Confirm directly before travel.
