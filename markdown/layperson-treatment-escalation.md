# PFIC4 treatment escalation guide for family discussion

Reviewed: 2026-05-24

This is a general research summary for discussing PFIC4 treatment escalation with a hepatology, genetics, transplant, or rare-disease team. It is not medical advice and should not be used to change treatment without a clinician.

Scope: this packet is primarily about confirmed PFIC4/TJP2. Treatments used in other PFIC types, Alagille syndrome, or broader cholestasis are included only when they help frame PFIC4 decisions.

## Plain-English bottom line

Treatment escalation means the care team moves step by step when PFIC4 symptoms, bile-acid levels, growth, vitamin levels, liver scarring, or cancer-surveillance concerns are not controlled well enough.

PFIC4 is caused by `TJP2` changes that affect tight junctions, the seals between liver cells. Approved PFIC medicines can lower bile-acid recycling and help itching, but they do not fix the gene. That is why the team should track both how the patient feels and what the labs and imaging show.

## The usual escalation ladder

| Step | When it enters the conversation | What it is trying to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline monitoring | At diagnosis or suspected PFIC4 | Understand current severity and set a comparison point. |
| Nutrition and vitamins | Early, not only after things get worse | Prevent poor growth, vitamin deficiency, bleeding risk, and bone problems. |
| Itch and sleep support | Whenever itch disrupts sleep, skin, school, activity, or family life | Reduce scratching and improve rest while liver-directed treatment is assessed. |
| IBAT inhibitor medicines | When PFIC/PFIC4 itch or bile-acid burden is significant and the team thinks bile-acid recycling can be lowered | Reduce bile-acid reabsorption from the intestine so more bile acids leave in stool. |
| Biliary diversion or ileal exclusion | If medicine is not enough and the liver is still a reasonable surgical candidate | Surgically reduce bile-acid recycling. |
| Liver transplant evaluation | If liver disease progresses, cancer is suspected, or symptoms/growth/nutrition remain severe despite other options | Replace the liver when risk or suffering becomes too high for other approaches. |

## What the team watches before escalating

Bring visits back to objective markers. A patient can feel better while some lab risks remain, or labs can improve while itch still ruins sleep.

| Area | What to track |
| --- | --- |
| Itch and sleep | Daily itch score, nighttime wakeups, bleeding or open skin, infection, school/daycare/activity impact. |
| Bile acids | Serum bile acids and how they change after treatment starts or changes. |
| Liver injury | Bilirubin, AST, ALT, and GGT. |
| Liver function | INR and albumin. INR can also reflect vitamin K deficiency, so the team has to interpret it carefully. |
| Portal hypertension clues | Platelets, spleen size, ultrasound/Doppler findings, ascites, or varices if checked. |
| Growth and nutrition | Weight, height, BMI or weight-for-length, appetite, stool burden, diarrhea, dehydration. |
| Vitamins | Vitamins A, D, E, and K, often with INR as part of vitamin K/liver-function interpretation. |
| Scarring and imaging | Ultrasound, elastography/FibroScan or liver stiffness, MRI/MRCP if needed. |
| Liver cancer surveillance | AFP blood test and imaging plan, especially if fibrosis/cirrhosis or PFIC4-specific concern is present. |

## When IBAT inhibitors enter the conversation

IBAT inhibitors include maralixibat/Livmarli and odevixibat/Bylvay. They block bile-acid reabsorption in the last part of the small intestine. The goal is for more bile acids to leave the body in stool and less to cycle back to the liver.

For PFIC4, the important caveats are:

- This is not gene repair. It does not fix `TJP2` tight junctions.
- It can still help if enough bile acids reach the intestine for recycling to be interrupted.
- Maralixibat has direct PFIC4 inclusion in the MARCH-PFIC trial, but the PFIC4 group was small.
- Odevixibat is PFIC-labeled, but its main randomized trial was PFIC1/PFIC2, not PFIC4.
- Both drugs need monitoring for diarrhea/dehydration, liver tests, fat-soluble vitamins, and signs of liver decompensation.

The team may judge response by itch, sleep, serum bile acids, bilirubin, growth, vitamin levels, and whether fibrosis or portal-hypertension markers are stable.

## When supportive itch medicines enter the conversation

Supportive itch medicines can enter early, especially if scratching causes poor sleep, bleeding skin, missed school/daycare, or major family stress. They can also be used when IBAT inhibitors are not available, only partly work, or cause side effects.

Common discussion items include:

- UDCA/ursodiol: may help bile flow or liver biochemistry in some cholestatic disease, but may not control severe PFIC itch alone.
- Rifampin: can help cholestatic itch in some patients, but liver tests and drug interactions matter.
- Cholestyramine, colesevelam, or colestipol: bind bile acids in the gut; timing matters because they can interfere with other medicines and vitamins.
- Naltrexone or related opioid-pathway medicines: sometimes used for difficult cholestatic itch under specialist care.
- Sertraline: sometimes used for cholestatic itch through central itch pathways; pediatric evidence is limited.
- Hydroxyzine or diphenhydramine: may help sleep but usually do not solve bile-acid itch by themselves.
- Ondansetron or phenobarbital: selected or older options with weaker evidence.

For a deeper comparison of lower-cost options with IBAT inhibitors, see [cheap/generic options compared with IBAT inhibitors](cheap-generic-options-vs-ibat.md). The short version is that bile-acid binders, also called bile-acid sequestrants, such as cholestyramine, colesevelam, and colestipol, are the closest cheap gut-based strategy, while rifampin, naltrexone, sertraline, and antihistamines mainly target itch or sleep pathways rather than the underlying bile-acid burden.

## When nutrition and vitamins enter the conversation

Nutrition is part of the first plan, not a backup plan. Cholestasis can make it harder to absorb fat and fat-soluble vitamins. IBAT inhibitors and bile-acid binding medicines, also called bile-acid sequestrants, such as cholestyramine, colesevelam, and colestipol, can also affect vitamin absorption.

Ask the team about:

- How often weight and height should be checked.
- Whether a liver dietitian should be involved.
- Whether medium-chain triglyceride oil, special formula, or extra calories are needed.
- How often vitamins A, D, E, and K should be checked.
- Whether INR changes are vitamin K, liver function, or both.
- Bone-health monitoring if vitamin D is low, fractures occur, or growth is poor.

## When biliary diversion enters the conversation

Biliary diversion is surgery to reduce bile-acid recycling. One version drains part of the bile outside the body through an opening; another reroutes bile internally. Ileal exclusion bypasses the part of the intestine where most bile acids are reabsorbed.

This usually enters the conversation when:

- Itch remains severe despite medicine.
- Serum bile acids remain high or the liver seems exposed to too much bile-acid burden.
- The team thinks surgery is safer or more useful before advanced cirrhosis.
- A PFIC center with surgical experience can review the case.

Important caveat: most biliary-diversion evidence is broader PFIC evidence, not large PFIC4-only evidence. It should be discussed with a center that understands PFIC genetics, surgical risks, nutrition, and transplant timing.

Risk warning: biliary diversion is major surgery, not a simple itch treatment. Published PFIC reviews report that some children improve, but some still need transplant later. Risks can include stoma leakage or blockage, skin problems around the stoma, high bile output, dehydration, low salt/electrolyte levels, diarrhea or frequent stools, poor absorption, infection or cholangitis, bile leak, repeat surgery, return of itching, or worsening liver disease despite surgery. The key question is not only "can it reduce itching?" but "is this child's liver stage, nutrition, anatomy, and PFIC4 genetics a good fit for surgery now?"

Rare rescue/bridge option: albumin dialysis, sometimes called MARS, is different from ordinary kidney dialysis. It has been reported to temporarily improve severe cholestatic itching in some non-PFIC4 cholestasis cases and one PFIC3 pregnancy case, but there is no strong PFIC4-specific proof. If it ever comes up, it should be treated as a specialist-center bridge option, not a cure or a replacement for PFIC4 escalation planning.

## When liver transplant enters the conversation

Transplant evaluation does not always mean transplant happens immediately. It can mean the team wants a transplant center involved before an emergency.

It enters the conversation when there is concern for:

- Progressive fibrosis or cirrhosis.
- Portal hypertension, such as enlarged spleen, low platelets, varices, or ascites.
- Liver synthetic dysfunction, such as INR or albumin problems not explained by vitamin deficiency alone.
- Liver decompensation, such as ascites, variceal bleeding, or encephalopathy.
- Liver cancer concern, rising AFP, or a liver lesion.
- Severe itch, poor sleep, poor growth, or vitamin/nutrition problems despite optimized care.

PFIC4 has published liver cancer reports in children and adults. That does not give a reliable percentage risk for one patient, but it does mean the HCC surveillance plan should be explicit.

## Questions to bring to the next visit

- What exact findings would make you change the current treatment?
- What counts as a good IBAT response: itch score, serum bile acids, bilirubin, growth, or all of these?
- What counts as treatment failure or intolerance?
- How often should serum bile acids, bilirubin, AST/ALT/GGT, INR, albumin, platelets, vitamins, and growth be checked?
- What is the ultrasound, elastography, AFP, and HCC surveillance plan for PFIC4?
- At what point would you discuss biliary diversion?
- At what point would you involve a transplant center?
- Should a PFIC or genetic cholestasis center review the treatment plan?

## Evidence limits

- PFIC4-specific studies are small.
- IBAT inhibitors can reduce itch and bile-acid burden, but they are not proven to repair `TJP2`.
- Maralixibat has direct PFIC4 trial inclusion, but not a large PFIC4-only trial.
- Odevixibat has PFIC labeling and PFIC trial evidence, but the pivotal randomized trial was PFIC1/PFIC2.
- Biliary diversion and transplant timing need individualized expert review because genotype, fibrosis, nutrition, HCC concern, and quality of life all matter.
- Albumin dialysis/MARS has only limited bridge-style evidence outside PFIC4; it should not be treated as proven PFIC4 disease-modifying therapy.
