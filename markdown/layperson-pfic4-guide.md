## One-page summary

PFIC4 is a rare inherited cholestatic liver disease caused by disease-causing changes in `TJP2`, the gene for tight junction protein 2, also called ZO-2. In the liver, tight junctions help seal the spaces between liver cells around tiny bile channels. When TJP2 does not work well, bile acids can leak back into liver tissue instead of staying safely in the bile pathway. Bile acids are detergent-like and can irritate nerves, drive severe itching, and injure liver cells over time.

Maralixibat does not fix the `TJP2` tight-junction problem. It blocks bile acid reabsorption in the last part of the small intestine, so more bile acids leave in stool and less recirculates back to the liver. If a patient responds, that suggests reducing the bile-acid pool is helping symptoms and may be helping biochemical liver stress.

The other currently approved PFIC drug in the same class is odevixibat, brand name Bylvay. In the U.S. label reviewed here, Bylvay is indicated for pruritus in PFIC patients 3 months and older. Livmarli, brand name for maralixibat, is indicated for cholestatic pruritus in PFIC patients 12 months and older. Both are IBAT inhibitors. They are alternatives in the same general strategy, not cures.

## What causes PFIC4 besides genetics?

The root cause is the inherited `TJP2` variants. Outside of that, the disease burden appears to come mainly from a cycle:

- TJP2 dysfunction weakens the tight seal between liver cells.
- Bile acids can leak or reflux into liver tissue.
- Bile acids irritate nerves and contribute to intense itching.
- Chronic bile-acid exposure injures liver cells.
- Repeated injury can lead to fibrosis, cirrhosis, portal hypertension, liver failure, and in PFIC4, concern for hepatocellular carcinoma.

What does not appear to be the cause: PFIC4 is not known to be caused by parenting choices, diet, infection, or lifestyle. Diet and illness can affect day-to-day health, nutrition, hydration, and medication tolerance, but they are not the underlying cause.

Things that may influence severity or quality of life:

- The exact `TJP2` variants and whether there is remaining protein function.
- Bile-acid level and bilirubin control.
- Growth and nutrition status.
- Fat-soluble vitamin levels: A, D, E, K.
- Medication response and side effects, especially diarrhea and vitamin malabsorption with IBAT inhibitors.
- Presence or absence of fibrosis, portal hypertension, or cirrhosis.
- Sleep disruption and skin injury from scratching.

## Drugs

### Maralixibat (Livmarli)

Maralixibat is an IBAT inhibitor used for PFIC-directed itch treatment. It is not a genetic cure. A response is meaningful because it suggests that lowering the recirculating bile-acid pool is helping.

- Class: IBAT inhibitor.
- Mechanism: blocks bile acid reabsorption from the terminal ileum.
- U.S. label: treatment of cholestatic pruritus in PFIC patients 12 months and older.
- Typical PFIC label dose: titrated toward 570 mcg/kg twice daily before meals as tolerated.
- Main risks to monitor: liver tests, diarrhea/dehydration, fat-soluble vitamin deficiency, abdominal pain, blood in stool, fractures/bleeding concerns, and propylene glycol toxicity risk in children under 5 using solution.
- Important caveat: it is not recommended for a subgroup of PFIC2 patients with nonfunctional or absent BSEP protein. That caveat is PFIC2-specific, not PFIC4-specific.

Detailed drug page: [Livmarli / maralixibat details](livmarli-maralixibat.md)

### Odevixibat (Bylvay)

Odevixibat is the main PFIC-specific alternative to ask about if maralixibat is not effective, is not tolerated, or the team thinks another IBAT inhibitor is worth trying.

- Class: IBAT inhibitor, same broad class as maralixibat.
- Mechanism: blocks bile acid reabsorption from the terminal ileum.
- U.S. label: treatment of pruritus in PFIC patients 3 months and older.
- Typical label starting dose: 40 mcg/kg once daily with a morning meal, with escalation if itch does not improve after 3 months.
- Main risks to monitor: liver tests, diarrhea/dehydration, fat-soluble vitamin deficiency, abdominal pain, vomiting.
- Important caveat: it is not recommended for a subgroup of PFIC2 patients with nonfunctional or absent BSEP protein. That caveat is PFIC2-specific, not PFIC4-specific.

Detailed drug page: [Bylvay / odevixibat details](bylvay-odevixibat.md)

### Supportive or off-label pruritus drugs

These are commonly discussed in PFIC/cholestatic pruritus, but most are not PFIC4-specific disease-modifying drugs:

- Ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA, also called ursodiol): a hydrophilic bile acid. It may improve bile flow/liver biochemistry in some cholestatic diseases, but benefit is variable in PFIC.
- Rifampin: can help cholestatic itch in some patients, but it is not reliable for everyone. It also needs liver-test monitoring because it can cause liver injury and has drug interactions.
- Cholestyramine, colesevelam, colestipol: bind bile acids in the gut; can interfere with other medicines and fat-soluble vitamins, so timing matters.
- Naltrexone: opioid antagonist sometimes used for severe cholestatic itch; can cause withdrawal-like symptoms in some settings.
- Sertraline: sometimes used for cholestatic itch through central itch-pathway effects; pediatric evidence is limited.
- Antihistamines such as hydroxyzine or diphenhydramine: may help sleep but often do not solve cholestatic itch by themselves.
- Ondansetron or phenobarbital: sometimes used historically; evidence is less strong.

## Improving quality of life

### Itching and sleep

Bring objective data to visits. A simple daily log can be more useful than memory:

- Morning itch score and nighttime itch score from 0 to 4.
- Sleep: number of wakeups and whether scratching caused waking.
- Skin injury: bleeding, open areas, infection concerns.
- Missed school/daycare or activity limitations.
- Diarrhea, abdominal pain, vomiting, or dehydration symptoms.

Non-drug measures can reduce skin damage even when they do not treat the liver cause:

- Keep nails short.
- Use bland moisturizers/emollients.
- Avoid hot baths and overheating, which can worsen itch.
- Use soft sleep clothing and bedding if scratching is causing injury.
- Ask whether sedating antihistamines are appropriate for sleep disruption, recognizing they usually do not treat bile-acid itch directly.

### Nutrition, growth, and vitamins

PFIC can impair fat absorption. Ask the team what schedule they want for:

- Weight, height, BMI or weight-for-length.
- Fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E, and K.
- INR as a vitamin K and liver synthetic-function signal.
- Bone health if vitamin D is low, fractures occur, or growth is affected.
- Medium-chain triglyceride support or higher-calorie nutrition if growth is lagging.

### Liver scarring, portal hypertension, and liver failure

Ask the hepatologist what their surveillance plan is for:

- Liver tests: AST, ALT, bilirubin, GGT, albumin, INR.
- Serum bile acids.
- Platelets and spleen size as portal-hypertension clues.
- Ultrasound and/or elastography/FibroScan for fibrosis and portal hypertension.
- AFP and liver imaging for HCC surveillance, especially if there is significant fibrosis/cirrhosis or if the clinician believes PFIC4 warrants closer screening.

Urgent symptoms to ask the care team about ahead of time:

- New abdominal swelling, vomiting blood, black stools, confusion, severe sleepiness, new easy bruising/bleeding, fever with worsening liver symptoms, dehydration from diarrhea, rapidly worsening jaundice, or severe abdominal pain.

### When medicine is not enough

If medications do not control itch or liver disease progresses, hepatology teams may discuss:

- Surgical biliary diversion: reduces bile acid recirculation by rerouting bile flow. It may help pruritus in some PFIC patients, especially before advanced cirrhosis.
- Liver transplant: considered for end-stage liver disease, liver cancer, or pruritus/growth failure that remains severe despite medical and surgical options.

## Questions to bring to hepatology

- Are the `TJP2` variants predicted to be loss-of-function, missense, splice-site, or another type?
- Is there evidence of residual TJP2 function from genetic reports or literature?
- How do serum bile acids, bilirubin, INR, albumin, platelets, growth, and itch scores change with treatment?
- What threshold would make you consider odevixibat instead of maralixibat?
- What is the monitoring plan for fat-soluble vitamins and bone health?
- Should PFIC4 HCC screening be done every 6 months, even without established cirrhosis, or only with significant fibrosis/cirrhosis?
- What findings would trigger transplant-center discussion?
- Are there PFIC or genetic cholestasis centers/trials worth consulting?
