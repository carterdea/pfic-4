## Working disease model

PFIC4 is TJP2/ZO-2-associated genetic cholestatic liver disease, usually autosomal recessive. The clinically relevant mechanism is not a canalicular transporter pump defect in the same sense as BSEP deficiency. TJP2 is a tight-junction scaffolding protein. Reported pathobiology includes impaired canalicular tight-junction integrity and mislocalization/failure of claudin-1 at the bile-canalicular membrane, allowing paracellular reflux/leakage of bile constituents into hepatic parenchyma. This offers a plausible reason PFIC4 may respond to IBAT inhibition: bile-acid secretion into bile and delivery to the intestine can be preserved enough for interruption of enterohepatic recirculation to reduce hepatic bile-acid load.

Expected clinical pattern:

- Low or normal GGT cholestasis with elevated serum bile acids, AST/ALT and bilirubin variable.
- Severe pruritus can be the dominant morbidity.
- Phenotype is variable: mild/recurrent cholestasis to early progressive disease, portal hypertension, cirrhosis, liver failure, and HCC.
- Genotype-phenotype signal exists but is incomplete: truncating/canonical splice biallelic variants and absent protein expression have been associated with more severe courses; missense/frame deletions may retain residual function and milder disease in some series.
- Extrahepatic findings have been reported because TJP2 is broadly expressed, including respiratory, neurologic, and hearing findings.

### Maralixibat (Livmarli)

U.S. label evidence reviewed:

- Indication: cholestatic pruritus in PFIC patients 12 months and older.
- Mechanism: reversible IBAT inhibition in terminal ileum, decreasing reabsorption of bile acids.
- PFIC dosing label: 570 mcg/kg twice daily before a meal, with titration; oral solution 19 mg/mL for PFIC and tablets for patients >=25 kg who can swallow tablets.
- Trial 2/NCT03905330: 26-week randomized placebo-controlled PFIC trial. Label states efficacy population included 64 patients with biallelic pathogenic variants: non-truncated PFIC2 n=31; PFIC1 n=13; PFIC3 n=9; PFIC4 n=7; PFIC6 n=4.
- Baseline stable UDCA and rifampin were common in the trial.
- Pruritus effect in label: average morning ItchRO(Obs) change from baseline to weeks 15-26 was -1.8 with maralixibat vs -0.6 with placebo; difference -1.2, p<0.0001.
- Serum bile acids decreased as early as week 2 in most patients, with fluctuation but maintained reduction during treatment.
- Limitation: not recommended in PFIC2 BSEP3/nonfunctional or absent BSEP subgroup.
- Key safety monitoring: hepatotoxicity, GI adverse reactions/diarrhea/dehydration, fat-soluble vitamin deficiency, fractures/bleeding, propylene glycol toxicity risk in children <5 years using solution.
- Contraindication: prior or active hepatic decompensation events.

Interpretation for PFIC4: trial included PFIC4, but n=7 is small. The label supports PFIC use across the included genotypes except the PFIC2 BSEP3 caveat. Clinical response in any individual patient would be meaningful, but not proof of long-term disease modification.

### Odevixibat (Bylvay)

U.S. label evidence reviewed:

- Indication: pruritus in PFIC patients 3 months and older.
- Mechanism: reversible IBAT inhibition in terminal ileum, decreasing reabsorption of bile acids.
- PFIC starting dose: 40 mcg/kg once daily in the morning with a meal; if no pruritus improvement after 3 months, may increase in 40 mcg/kg increments up to 120 mcg/kg once daily, max 6 mg/day.
- Trial 1/NCT03566238: 24-week randomized placebo-controlled trial in 62 pediatric patients age 6 months to 17 years with molecular PFIC1 or PFIC2 and baseline pruritus.
- Label clinical effect: greater improvement in pruritus vs placebo; mean percentage of assessments scored no/little scratching 35.4% at 40 mcg/kg/day and 30.1% at 120 mcg/kg/day vs 13.2% placebo.
- Serum bile acids reduced from baseline within 4-8 weeks vs placebo and generally maintained over 24 weeks.
- Limitation: not recommended in PFIC2 patients with variants causing nonfunctional or absent BSEP.
- Key safety monitoring: hepatotoxicity, diarrhea/dehydration, fat-soluble vitamin deficiency, fracture/bleeding signals, liver-test abnormalities, abdominal pain, vomiting.
- Contraindication: prior or active hepatic decompensation events.

Interpretation for PFIC4: the pivotal placebo-controlled odevixibat trial was PFIC1/PFIC2, but approval is for PFIC pruritus. Later open-label and review literature includes broader PFIC experience; direct PFIC4 evidence remains limited compared with MARCH-PFIC's PFIC4 inclusion for maralixibat.

## Non-IBAT medical options

These are best considered adjunctive, bridge, or symptom-targeted therapies. Most are off-label in PFIC4 and not proven to prevent progression in PFIC4.

| Option | Rationale | Key caveats |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UDCA / ursodiol / ursodeoxycholic acid | Hydrophilic bile acid; may improve bile flow and bile-acid composition. | Variable PFIC response; not a reliable antipruritic alone in severe disease. Cholic-acid products exist for bile-acid synthesis disorders, not typical PFIC4. |
| Rifampin | Induces detoxification/excretion pathways; commonly used for cholestatic pruritus | Mixed individual response. Hepatotoxicity and drug interactions; monitor liver tests |
| Cholestyramine/colesevelam/colestipol | Bind bile acids in gut | Palatability, constipation, malabsorption; separate from IBAT inhibitors and other drugs |
| Naltrexone | Central opioid pathway modulation of cholestatic itch | Withdrawal-like symptoms; pediatric use needs specialist oversight |
| Sertraline | Central itch perception modulation | Limited pediatric data; hepatic metabolism and behavioral/GI side effects |
| Hydroxyzine/diphenhydramine | Sedation/sleep support | Usually insufficient as monotherapy for cholestatic itch |
| Ondansetron/phenobarbital | Historical/selected use | Less convincing efficacy base |
| Nutrition/FSV replacement | Treats malabsorption consequences | Needs lab-guided dosing; monitor INR, A/D/E levels, growth and bone health |

Investigational or adjacent drugs: linerixibat and volixibat are IBAT inhibitors studied in other cholestatic pruritus settings, such as PBC/PSC/ICP, but they are not established PFIC4 therapies in the reviewed sources. FXR agonists, 4-phenylbutyrate, and chaperone approaches are mechanistically interesting in selected cholestatic diseases, but not currently a practical PFIC4 standard based on this search.

## Monitoring and response questions

Potential treatment-response metrics:

- Daily caregiver/patient pruritus score using a consistent 0-4 scale such as ItchRO/PRUCISION-style tracking.
- Sleep disruption, excoriations, bleeding, school/activity impairment.
- Serum bile acids. Several PFIC publications use large percentage reduction and/or threshold-based response definitions. Ask which threshold the treating center uses for PFIC4.
- Total/direct bilirubin, AST, ALT, GGT.
- INR, albumin, platelets, CBC.
- Fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E and INR/vitamin K status.
- Growth velocity, nutritional intake, stool burden/diarrhea.
- Liver stiffness/elastography and ultrasound.

Monitoring frequency from PFIC opinion literature commonly centers on clinical assessment, liver biochemistry, fat-soluble vitamins, serum bile acids, growth, pruritus and QoL every 3-6 months, with liver ultrasound and stiffness assessment every 6-12 months. For established cirrhosis, ultrasound plus AFP every 6 months is consistent with HCC surveillance practice. Because PFIC4 has published HCC cases even in young patients and GeneReviews lists PFIC4 surveillance "For HCC", clarify whether the treating center screens all PFIC4 patients at a 6-month interval or risk-stratifies by fibrosis/cirrhosis.

## Disease-modifying uncertainty

Maralixibat and odevixibat clearly target pruritus and serum bile-acid burden. Whether early durable bile-acid reduction prevents fibrosis progression and transplant in PFIC4 specifically remains less certain because PFIC4 cohorts are small. That said, the biologic rationale for IBAT response is plausible in PFIC4, and MARCH-PFIC included PFIC4 patients under the broader all-PFIC efficacy signal.

Clinical implication: when a patient responds symptomatically, the key question is not only "is the itch better?" but "are serum bile acids, bilirubin, growth, sleep, vitamin status, and fibrosis/portal-hypertension markers also improving or stable?"

## Escalation pathways

Consider specialist discussion of biliary diversion or transplant evaluation when any of the following emerge despite optimized medical management:

- Intractable pruritus with major sleep/skin/QoL impairment.
- Growth failure or refractory nutritional/vitamin deficiency.
- Progressive fibrosis/cirrhosis, portal hypertension, or synthetic dysfunction.
- Decompensation: ascites, variceal bleeding, encephalopathy.
- HCC concern or confirmed malignancy.

Surgical biliary diversion has historical benefit in selected PFIC patients by interrupting enterohepatic circulation, especially before advanced cirrhosis. Liver transplant remains the definitive option for end-stage disease, HCC, or refractory symptoms when medical/surgical supportive care fails. PFIC4 transplant series are limited but reported post-transplant outcomes are generally favorable, with high HCC burden at transplant in systematic review data.

## Unresolved questions for the treating team

- What are the exact `TJP2` variants, zygosity, predicted effect, and any known residual function?
- Was baseline histology or elastography consistent with fibrosis? If so, what stage?
- What has changed with IBAT inhibitor therapy: ItchRO/sleep, serum bile acids, bilirubin, growth, vitamins, INR, albumin, platelets?
- Are there medication interactions with cholestyramine/colesevelam, vitamins, or other drugs that could reduce maralixibat exposure or vitamin absorption?
- What is the center's PFIC4-specific HCC surveillance interval?
- What threshold defines inadequate response and triggers odevixibat trial, combination/supportive therapy, biliary diversion, or transplant-center referral?
- Is second-opinion review at a pediatric genetic cholestasis/PFIC center appropriate?
