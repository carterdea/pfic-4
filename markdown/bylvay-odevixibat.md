## Core facts

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Brand name | Bylvay |
| Generic / active ingredient | Odevixibat |
| Salt / active ingredient form | Odevixibat sesquihydrate. Label strengths are expressed as odevixibat. |
| Drug class | Ileal bile acid transporter (IBAT) inhibitor |
| U.S. PFIC indication | Treatment of pruritus in patients 3 months and older with progressive familial intrahepatic cholestasis (PFIC). |
| PFIC limitation | May not be effective in PFIC2 patients with `ABCB11` variants causing nonfunctional or absent BSEP protein. This limitation is PFIC2-specific, not PFIC4-specific. |
| PFIC4 evidence | Bylvay is PFIC-labeled, but the pivotal placebo-controlled PFIC trial was in PFIC1/PFIC2, not PFIC4. PFIC4-specific evidence is extrapolated. |

## Ingredients and dosage forms

| Product | Active ingredient | Strengths | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Oral pellets | Odevixibat sesquihydrate, strength expressed as odevixibat | 200 mcg, 600 mcg | Intended for patients weighing less than 19.5 kg. |
| Capsules | Odevixibat sesquihydrate, strength expressed as odevixibat | 400 mcg, 1200 mcg | Intended for patients weighing 19.5 kg or more. |

Inactive ingredients listed in the label include hypromellose and microcrystalline cellulose. Oral pellet shells contain hypromellose, titanium dioxide, and yellow iron oxide. Capsule shells contain hypromellose, red iron oxide, titanium dioxide, and yellow iron oxide.

## How it works

Odevixibat reversibly inhibits the ileal bile acid transporter in the terminal ileum. This decreases reabsorption of bile acids back into circulation and can lower serum bile acids. The label notes that the complete mechanism for improving pruritus is not fully known.

For PFIC4, the rationale is similar to maralixibat: if bile acids reach the intestine, interrupting their reabsorption may reduce the recirculating bile-acid pool. The evidence base is less directly PFIC4-specific than maralixibat because the pivotal randomized trial did not include PFIC4.

## PFIC dosing from U.S. label

- Starting PFIC dose: 40 mcg/kg orally once daily in the morning with a meal.
- If pruritus does not improve after 3 months: increase in 40 mcg/kg increments up to 120 mcg/kg once daily.
- Maximum daily PFIC dose: 6 mg/day.
- Oral pellets are intended for patients under 19.5 kg.
- Capsules are intended for patients 19.5 kg or more.

## Major warnings and monitoring

| Issue | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Hepatotoxicity | Bylvay treatment is associated with potential drug-induced liver injury. Baseline liver tests and frequent monitoring during the first 6-8 months are label recommendations. |
| Diarrhea / dehydration | Diarrhea is common and can require interruption, dose reduction, hydration support, or discontinuation if persistent. |
| Fat-soluble vitamin deficiency | IBAT inhibition can worsen absorption of vitamins A, D, E, and K. Monitor baseline and follow-up vitamin levels and INR. |
| Fracture / bleeding | The label flags fracture and bleeding concerns in the setting of fat-soluble vitamin deficiency. |
| Hepatic decompensation | Contraindicated in patients with prior or active hepatic decompensation events such as variceal hemorrhage, ascites, or hepatic encephalopathy. |
| Cirrhosis / portal hypertension | Safety and effectiveness have not been established in decompensated cirrhosis; patients with compensated cirrhosis or portal hypertension need more frequent monitoring. |

## Common PFIC adverse reactions

PFIC Trial 1 common reactions included:

- Liver test abnormalities
- Diarrhea / frequent bowel movements
- Abdominal pain
- Vomiting
- Fat-soluble vitamin deficiency
- Increased bilirubin
- Splenomegaly
- Cholelithiasis
- Dehydration
- Fracture

Trial 1 label rates included diarrhea in 10% placebo, 39% at 40 mcg/kg/day, and 21% at 120 mcg/kg/day. Transaminase increases occurred in 5% placebo, 13% at 40 mcg/kg/day, and 21% at 120 mcg/kg/day.

Postmarketing reports include GI hemorrhage, gingival hemorrhage, liver transplant, increased GGT, decreased hemoglobin, subdural hemorrhage, and epistaxis; frequency cannot be estimated from voluntary reports.

## Drug interactions

- Bile acid binding resins such as cholestyramine, colesevelam, or colestipol may bind odevixibat in the gut and reduce efficacy. Give Bylvay at least 4 hours before or 4 hours after these medicines.
- Odevixibat is a P-gp substrate. Itraconazole increased exposure, but the label says this is not expected to be clinically significant.
- No clinically relevant CYP3A effect is expected.

## Pharmacokinetics / metabolism

- Odevixibat is minimally absorbed.
- In pediatric PFIC patients, most plasma samples were below quantification; measurable levels ranged 0.06-0.72 ng/mL.
- No accumulation was seen with once-daily dosing.
- Protein binding is greater than 99% in vitro.
- Mean half-life after a 7.2 mg adult dose was about 2.36 hours.
- In vitro metabolism: mono-hydroxylation.
- Excretion after a radiolabeled dose was mainly fecal: 82.9% in feces, 97% unchanged; less than 0.002% in urine.

## Trial evidence

This table focuses on PFIC-relevant odevixibat trials. It does not list every odevixibat study in other cholestatic diseases such as Alagille syndrome or biliary atresia.

| Trial | Design | Population | PFIC4 relevance | Main evidence signal | Practical read |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PEDFIC 1 / Trial 1 / NCT03566238 | Phase 3, 24-week, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial | 62 pediatric patients with molecular PFIC1 or PFIC2 and baseline pruritus | Did not include PFIC4 | Positive pruritus assessments were 55% with combined odevixibat vs 30% placebo. Serum bile-acid response was 33% with combined odevixibat vs 0% placebo. | Best placebo-controlled odevixibat PFIC evidence, but PFIC4 use is extrapolated from PFIC1/PFIC2 and the broad PFIC label. |
| PEDFIC 2 / NCT03659916 | Phase 3, open-label extension and broader PFIC enrollment | Patients from PEDFIC 1 plus newly enrolled PFIC patients; EU results report 116 participants through a 2024 cutoff | Extension/broader PFIC program included "other PFIC" subgroups such as PFIC3, PFIC4, PFIC6, and episodic PFIC, but not randomized PFIC4 evidence | Interim publication reported mean serum bile-acid and pruritus improvements through 24 weeks of PEDFIC 2; EU product information reports 72-week data with serum bile-acid responder rates sustained in some groups. | Helpful longer-term and broader-genotype evidence, but open-label and still weaker than PFIC4-specific randomized data. |
| A4250-003 dose-finding study | Phase 2 dose-finding study in pediatric cholestatic liver disease | Included pediatric cholestatic liver disease patients, including PFIC, per EMA product information | Supportive background only | Used to support dose selection and early efficacy/safety understanding. | Not the main evidence for PFIC4 decisions. |

PFIC4 read: Bylvay is U.S.-labeled for PFIC pruritus broadly, but the pivotal randomized trial did not include PFIC4. It should not be presented as having PFIC4-specific randomized efficacy data.
