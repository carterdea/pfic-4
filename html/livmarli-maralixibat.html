How It Works

Maralixibat reversibly inhibits the ileal bile acid transporter in the terminal ileum. This decreases intestinal bile acid reabsorption, increases fecal bile acid loss, and can lower serum bile acids. The label notes that the complete mechanism for reducing cholestatic pruritus is not fully understood.

For PFIC4, the rationale is plausible because the primary problem is tight-junction leakage rather than complete absence of bile-acid export into bile. If bile acids still reach the intestine, blocking reabsorption can reduce the recirculating bile-acid pool.

PFIC Dosing

  • Use Livmarli 19 mg/mL oral solution or tablets for PFIC.
  • Recommended PFIC target dose: 570 mcg/kg twice daily, taken 30 minutes before a meal.
  • Titration: start 285 mcg/kg once daily in the morning, then increase to 285 mcg/kg twice daily, 428 mcg/kg twice daily, and then 570 mcg/kg twice daily as tolerated.
  • Maximum daily dose: 38 mg/day for oral solution or 40 mg/day for tablets.
  • Tablets can be used in patients at least 25 kg who can swallow tablets.

Warnings And Monitoring

IssueWhy it matters
HepatotoxicityLiver test worsening and drug-induced liver injury have been reported. Baseline liver tests and frequent monitoring during the first 6-8 months are label recommendations.
GI adverse reactionsDiarrhea and abdominal pain are common; persistent symptoms, blood in stool, vomiting, dehydration, or fever may require dose reduction/interruption.
Fat-soluble vitamin deficiencyIBAT inhibition can worsen absorption of vitamins A, D, E, and K. Monitor vitamin levels and INR; supplement if deficient.
Fracture / bleedingThe label links these concerns to fat-soluble vitamin deficiency, especially vitamin D and K issues.
Propylene glycol exposureThe oral solution contains propylene glycol. Children under 5 years are at highest risk for toxicity, so total daily exposure matters.
Hepatic decompensationContraindicated in patients with prior or active hepatic decompensation events such as variceal hemorrhage, ascites, or hepatic encephalopathy.

Adverse Reactions

In the PFIC trial label summary, common adverse reactions included diarrhea, fat-soluble vitamin deficiency, abdominal pain, liver test abnormalities, hematochezia/rectal hemorrhage, and bone fractures.

Trial 2 rates highlighted in the label/subagent review included diarrhea 57.4% with Livmarli vs 19.6% with placebo, abdominal pain 27.7% vs 15.2%, increased ALT/AST 17% vs 6.5%, hematochezia/rectal hemorrhage 8.5% vs 2.2%, and bone fractures 6.4% vs 0.

Interactions

  • Bile acid binding resins such as cholestyramine, colesevelam, or colestipol may bind maralixibat in the gut. Give Livmarli at least 4 hours before or 4 hours after these medicines.
  • Maralixibat inhibits OATP2B1 in vitro. The label notes decreased absorption of OATP2B1 substrates, such as some statins, cannot be ruled out.

Pharmacokinetics

Maralixibat has minimal systemic absorption; pediatric ALGS/PFIC plasma levels were below quantification in most samples. Median Tmax after a 30 mg adult solution dose was about 0.75 hours. Plasma protein binding is about 91% in vitro. Mean half-life after 30 mg in adults was about 1.6 hours. Elimination is mainly fecal.
