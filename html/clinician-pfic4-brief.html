Working Disease Model

PFIC4 is TJP2/ZO-2-associated genetic cholestatic liver disease, usually autosomal recessive. The clinically relevant mechanism is not a canalicular transporter pump defect in the same sense as BSEP deficiency. TJP2 is a tight-junction scaffolding protein. Reported pathobiology includes impaired canalicular tight-junction integrity and claudin-1 mislocalization/failure at the bile-canalicular membrane, allowing paracellular reflux/leakage of bile constituents into hepatic parenchyma.

This offers a plausible reason PFIC4 may respond to IBAT inhibition: bile-acid secretion into bile and delivery to the intestine can be preserved enough for interruption of enterohepatic recirculation to reduce hepatic bile-acid load.

Monitoring And Response Questions

  • Daily caregiver/patient pruritus score using a consistent 0-4 scale.
  • Sleep disruption, excoriations, bleeding, school/activity impairment.
  • Serum bile acids, bilirubin, AST, ALT, GGT, INR, albumin, platelets, CBC.
  • Fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E and INR/vitamin K status.
  • Growth velocity, nutrition, stool burden/diarrhea, liver stiffness, ultrasound, AFP.

Disease-modifying Uncertainty

Maralixibat and odevixibat clearly target pruritus and serum bile-acid burden. Whether early durable bile-acid reduction prevents fibrosis progression and transplant in PFIC4 specifically remains less certain because PFIC4 cohorts are small.

Unresolved Questions

  • What are the exact TJP2 variants, zygosity, predicted effect, and known residual function?
  • What has changed with IBAT inhibitor therapy: ItchRO/sleep, serum bile acids, bilirubin, growth, vitamins, INR, albumin, platelets?
  • What is the center's PFIC4-specific HCC surveillance interval?
  • What threshold defines inadequate response and triggers odevixibat trial, biliary diversion, or transplant-center referral?
