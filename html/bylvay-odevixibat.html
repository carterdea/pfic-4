How It Works

Odevixibat reversibly inhibits the ileal bile acid transporter in the terminal ileum. This decreases reabsorption of bile acids back into circulation and can lower serum bile acids. The label notes that the complete mechanism for improving pruritus is not fully known.

For PFIC4, the rationale is similar to maralixibat, but the evidence base is less directly PFIC4-specific because the pivotal randomized trial did not include PFIC4.

PFIC Dosing

  • Starting PFIC dose: 40 mcg/kg orally once daily in the morning with a meal.
  • If pruritus does not improve after 3 months: increase in 40 mcg/kg increments up to 120 mcg/kg once daily.
  • Maximum daily PFIC dose: 6 mg/day.
  • Oral pellets are intended for patients under 19.5 kg; capsules are intended for patients 19.5 kg or more.

Warnings And Monitoring

IssueWhy it matters
HepatotoxicityBylvay treatment is associated with potential drug-induced liver injury. Baseline liver tests and frequent monitoring during the first 6-8 months are label recommendations.
Diarrhea / dehydrationDiarrhea is common and can require interruption, dose reduction, hydration support, or discontinuation if persistent.
Fat-soluble vitamin deficiencyIBAT inhibition can worsen absorption of vitamins A, D, E, and K. Monitor baseline and follow-up vitamin levels and INR.
Fracture / bleedingThe label flags fracture and bleeding concerns in the setting of fat-soluble vitamin deficiency.
Hepatic decompensationContraindicated in patients with prior or active hepatic decompensation events such as variceal hemorrhage, ascites, or hepatic encephalopathy.
Cirrhosis / portal hypertensionSafety and effectiveness have not been established in decompensated cirrhosis; patients with compensated cirrhosis or portal hypertension need more frequent monitoring.

Adverse Reactions

PFIC Trial 1 common reactions included liver test abnormalities, diarrhea/frequent bowel movements, abdominal pain, vomiting, fat-soluble vitamin deficiency, increased bilirubin, splenomegaly, cholelithiasis, dehydration, and fracture.

Trial 1 label rates included diarrhea in 10% placebo, 39% at 40 mcg/kg/day, and 21% at 120 mcg/kg/day. Transaminase increases occurred in 5% placebo, 13% at 40 mcg/kg/day, and 21% at 120 mcg/kg/day.

Postmarketing reports include GI hemorrhage, gingival hemorrhage, liver transplant, increased GGT, decreased hemoglobin, subdural hemorrhage, and epistaxis; frequency cannot be estimated from voluntary reports.

Interactions

  • Bile acid binding resins such as cholestyramine, colesevelam, or colestipol may bind odevixibat in the gut and reduce efficacy. Give Bylvay at least 4 hours before or 4 hours after these medicines.
  • Odevixibat is a P-gp substrate. Itraconazole increased exposure, but the label says this is not expected to be clinically significant.
  • No clinically relevant CYP3A effect is expected.

Pharmacokinetics

Odevixibat is minimally absorbed. In pediatric PFIC patients, most plasma samples were below quantification; measurable levels ranged 0.06-0.72 ng/mL. No accumulation was seen with once-daily dosing. Protein binding is greater than 99% in vitro. Mean half-life after a 7.2 mg adult dose was about 2.36 hours. Excretion is mainly fecal.
