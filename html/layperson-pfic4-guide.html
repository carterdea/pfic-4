One-page summary

PFIC4 is a rare inherited cholestatic liver disease caused by disease-causing changes in TJP2, the gene for tight junction protein 2, also called ZO-2. In the liver, tight junctions help seal the spaces between liver cells around tiny bile channels. When TJP2 does not work well, bile acids can leak back into liver tissue instead of staying safely in the bile pathway.

Maralixibat does not fix the TJP2 tight-junction problem. It blocks bile acid reabsorption in the last part of the small intestine, so more bile acids leave in stool and less recirculates back to the liver. If a patient responds, that suggests reducing the bile-acid pool is helping symptoms and may be helping biochemical liver stress.

Both maralixibat/Livmarli and odevixibat/Bylvay are IBAT inhibitors. They reduce bile-acid recirculation. They are treatment tools, not genetic cures.

What causes PFIC4 besides genetics?

  • TJP2 dysfunction weakens the tight seal between liver cells.
  • Bile acids can leak or reflux into liver tissue.
  • Bile acids irritate nerves and contribute to intense itching.
  • Chronic bile-acid exposure injures liver cells.
  • Repeated injury can lead to fibrosis, cirrhosis, portal hypertension, liver failure, and concern for hepatocellular carcinoma.

PFIC4 is not known to be caused by parenting choices, diet, infection, or lifestyle. Diet and illness can affect day-to-day health, nutrition, hydration, and medication tolerance, but they are not the underlying cause.

Drugs

Maralixibat (Livmarli) is an IBAT inhibitor used for PFIC-directed itch treatment. See the dedicated page: Livmarli / maralixibat drug details.

Odevixibat (Bylvay) is the main PFIC-specific alternative to ask about if maralixibat is not effective, is not tolerated, or the team thinks another IBAT inhibitor is worth trying. See the dedicated page: Bylvay / odevixibat drug details.

Improving quality of life

  • Track morning itch, nighttime itch, sleep wakeups, skin injury, missed activities, diarrhea, abdominal pain, vomiting, and dehydration symptoms.
  • Ask about fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E, and K; INR; bone health; growth; and nutrition support.
  • Ask about serum bile acids, bilirubin, liver tests, platelets, ultrasound, elastography/FibroScan, AFP, and HCC surveillance.

For a step-by-step review of what usually triggers IBAT inhibitor, supportive itch medicine, nutrition, biliary diversion, or transplant discussions, see PFIC4 treatment escalation guide for family discussion.

Questions to bring to hepatology

  • Are the TJP2 variants predicted to be loss-of-function, missense, splice-site, or another type?
  • How do serum bile acids, bilirubin, INR, albumin, platelets, growth, and itch scores change with treatment?
  • What threshold would make you consider odevixibat instead of maralixibat?
  • Should PFIC4 HCC screening be every 6 months or risk-stratified by fibrosis/cirrhosis?
  • What findings would trigger transplant-center discussion?
